A computer program is a sequence of instructions that tells a machine how to transform input into output. Good programs are written for two audiences: the computer, which needs precise and unambiguous steps, and the people who will read and change the code later. For that reason, experienced engineers favor clear names, small functions, and tests that describe the expected behavior. When a program grows, it is usually divided into modules that hide their internal details behind simple interfaces.

Water moves through the environment in a continuous cycle. Heat from the sun evaporates water from oceans, lakes, and rivers, and the vapor rises until it cools and condenses into clouds. When droplets in the clouds become heavy enough, they fall as rain or snow. Some of that water soaks into the ground and refills underground aquifers, while the rest flows across the surface into streams that eventually return to the sea.

To bake a simple loaf of bread, mix flour, water, salt, and yeast until the dough comes together. Knead it for about ten minutes, until it feels smooth and elastic, then cover the bowl and let it rise in a warm place for an hour or two. Shape the dough, let it rise again, and bake it in a hot oven until the crust is golden and the loaf sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom. Let it cool before slicing, or the inside will be gummy.

Exercise has benefits that reach far beyond physical strength. Regular walking, cycling, or swimming improves the health of the heart and lungs, helps regulate sleep, and can reduce stress and anxiety.
